Cost of Studying in the USA for Pakistani Students (2026–27 Guide)

Tuition, living costs, visa and SEVIS fees, health insurance and flights: what a US degree really costs a Pakistani student, and how families can lower the bill.

"How much will it cost?" is the first question almost every family asks us, and the honest answer is: it depends far more on which university you choose than on which country you are in. Two US Master's programs can differ by $40,000 a year.

This guide breaks the cost into its real parts so you can build your own budget. All figures are in US dollars and are typical ranges for the 2026–27 academic year. Your university's own figure is the one that counts, and it is printed on your I-20.

The number that matters most: cost of attendance

Every US university publishes an estimated cost of attendance for international students. It includes tuition, fees, housing, food, health insurance, books and personal expenses for one academic year. This is the amount you must show in your financial documents for the I-20 and the visa interview, so it is the right number to plan around.

Find it on the university's international admissions or "international student budget" page. If you cannot find it, email the admissions office and ask.

Tuition

Typical yearly tuition for international students:

  • Community colleges: about $8,000 to $15,000. Some students complete two years here, then transfer to a university to finish a Bachelor's degree.
  • Public (state) universities: about $25,000 to $45,000. International students pay the "out-of-state" rate.
  • Private universities: about $35,000 to $65,000 or more, but private universities also tend to give the largest scholarships.

A Master's degree is usually one and a half to two years. A Bachelor's is four years, so the total difference between a cheaper and a more expensive university is large.

Living costs

Housing, food, transport and daily costs vary by city:

  • Smaller towns and the Midwest or South: roughly $12,000 to $16,000 a year.
  • Big cities such as New York, Boston and the San Francisco Bay Area: $20,000 to $28,000 or more.

Sharing an apartment with other students and cooking at home are the two biggest ways students cut this number. Pakistani student associations on most large campuses help newcomers find shared housing.

Health insurance

Almost every university requires health insurance. University plans typically cost $1,500 to $4,000 a year. Do not skip it or try to go without: medical bills in the USA are extremely expensive.

One-time costs before you leave

  • English test: IELTS, TOEFL or Duolingo English Test, paid in rupees in Pakistan.
  • University application fees: usually $50 to $100 per university. Some universities waive the fee if you ask.
  • SEVIS I-901 fee: $350.
  • Visa application (MRV) fee: $185, paid before your interview.
  • Visa integrity fee: $250, a new fee created by US law in 2025 and charged when your visa is issued. Collection has been rolling out at different speeds at different consulates, so check the current position before your interview.
  • Flight to the USA: often $700 to $1,500 one way, depending on the season and how early you book.
  • First-month setup: housing deposit, bedding, a phone plan and winter clothes. Budget $1,500 to $2,500.

Good to know

Fees are paid directly by you to the university, the testing organisation or the US government. No consultant should ask you to pay these fees through them. At Hamraah, our prices are for guidance only and are listed on our pricing page.

Example budgets for one year

These are illustrations, not quotes.

Master's at a public university in a smaller city

  • Tuition and fees: $30,000
  • Living costs: $14,000
  • Health insurance: $2,500
  • Books and personal: $2,000
  • Total: about $48,500 a year

Master's at a private university in a large city

  • Tuition and fees: $55,000
  • Living costs: $24,000
  • Health insurance: $3,500
  • Books and personal: $2,500
  • Total: about $85,000 a year

To convert to rupees, multiply by the current exchange rate on the day you plan. Exchange rate changes can add a lot to the cost of a four-year degree, so families should plan with some margin.

Six ways to bring the cost down

  1. Apply for merit scholarships at the same time as admission. Many universities consider you automatically. Read our guide to scholarships for Pakistani students in the USA.
  2. Look for graduate assistantships. Teaching and research assistantships can cover tuition and pay a monthly stipend, especially in STEM PhD programs and some Master's programs.
  3. Choose universities in lower-cost areas. The same degree in a smaller city can save $8,000 to $12,000 a year in living costs.
  4. Consider community college first for a Bachelor's degree, then transfer.
  5. Work on campus. F-1 students can usually work up to 20 hours a week on campus during semesters. This helps with daily expenses, but you cannot rely on it to pay tuition, and the visa officer will not count it as funding.
  6. Finish on time. An extra semester adds a large cost, so plan your courses with your advisor from the start.

What about showing funds for the visa?

You need to show that the full first-year cost on your I-20 is available, and that the money has a clear, genuine source. The officer will also ask how later years will be paid.

Search "fully funded scholarship USA" and you will find hundreds of lists. Most of them repeat the same famous names, and many link to programs that are closed or not open to Pakistanis.

The truth is simpler and more encouraging: **most international students who study in the USA for less are not funded by one big named scholarship.** They are funded by the university itself, through merit scholarships, need-based aid or assistantships. Knowing where to look changes your whole university list.

## 1. Fulbright (Master's and PhD)

The Fulbright Degree program for Pakistan is run by USEFP, the United States Educational Foundation in Pakistan, and is one of the largest Fulbright programs in the world. It is fully funded: tuition, textbooks, airfare, a living stipend and health insurance.

- **Who:** Pakistani graduates applying for a Master's or PhD in the USA. Most fields are eligible, except clinical medicine. Recent cycles have given priority to science, technology and AI, trade and economics, and natural resource management.
- **When:** applications usually open early in the year with a spring deadline. For the 2027 program the deadline was 1 April 2026, so the next cycle is the one to prepare for now.
- **What to expect:** a competitive written application, tests, and an interview. Fulbright scholars return to Pakistan after their degree for a period of time, so your plan must lead back home.

Always check the current eligibility, test requirements and dates on the official [USEFP website](https://www.usefp.org), and apply only through it. USEFP does not charge an application fee through agents.

## 2. University merit scholarships

Many US universities give international students partial scholarships based on grades, test scores and the strength of the application. These typically range from a few thousand dollars a year to half of tuition or more.

- Some universities consider every admitted student automatically. Others need a separate scholarship application, often with an **earlier deadline** than admission.
- Private universities and smaller colleges often give larger awards than big public universities, so a private university can sometimes cost less in the end.
- The scholarship usually renews each year if you keep a minimum GPA.

The skill here is building a list of universities where your profile is strong enough to be in the top part of their applicants. That is where merit money goes.

## 3. Need-based financial aid (mainly Bachelor's)

A small number of very selective US universities meet the full financial need of every admitted student, including international students. Well-known examples include MIT, Harvard, Yale, Princeton, Amherst and Dartmouth. Many more colleges offer generous need-based aid to some international students.

This is extremely competitive, but it is real. Each year Pakistani students from ordinary middle-class families get into these places with full aid. You will need to submit your family's financial information, usually through the CSS Profile or the university's own form.

!!! Good to know
At universities that are not "need-blind" for international students, asking for aid can affect your chance of admission. Ask each university directly how it treats international applicants who request aid, so you can decide where it makes sense.

## 4. Graduate assistantships (Master's and PhD)

This is the single biggest source of funding for graduate students from Pakistan.

- **Research assistantships (RA):** you work in a professor's lab or project.
- **Teaching assistantships (TA):** you help teach undergraduate classes, grade or run labs.
- **Graduate assistantships (GA):** work in a university office or department.

A full assistantship usually means **tuition waived plus a monthly stipend**, often enough to live on. Most PhD students in science and engineering in the USA are funded this way. Master's students get assistantships less often, but it does happen, especially after the first semester.

How to improve your chances:

1. For a PhD, read the recent papers of two or three professors at each university and email them a short, specific message about your research interest before you apply.
2. Mention research experience, publications, teaching or lab work clearly in your CV.
3. Once you arrive, ask departments about openings early in the semester. Many TA positions are filled locally.

## 5. Other sources worth checking

- **HEC Pakistan** has run overseas scholarship schemes, mostly for PhD study in priority fields. Check [hec.gov.pk](https://www.hec.gov.pk) for any scheme currently open.
- **Professional bodies and foundations** in your field sometimes fund international students. Ask your department after you are admitted.
- **University departmental awards** are often listed only on the department's own website, not the main scholarship page.

## Scholarship red flags

- Anyone who asks for money to "secure" a scholarship.
- Websites that promise a scholarship before you have even applied to a university.
- "Fully funded" offers from universities you cannot find on official accreditation lists.

## Plan backwards from the deadline

US scholarship deadlines for Fall intake are often between **November and February**, nearly a year before you start. Fulbright usually closes in spring for the following year's intake.

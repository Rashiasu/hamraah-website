Germany is one of the few countries where an international student can earn a respected Bachelor's or Master's degree at a public university without paying tuition. For many Pakistani families who find the USA or UK too expensive, it is the most realistic route to a degree abroad.

"Free" does not mean no cost, though. This guide explains what you actually pay, what you need to show for the visa, and who Germany suits best.

## Is it really tuition-free?

At most **public universities** in Germany, international students pay no tuition for Bachelor's degrees and for most Master's degrees. There are important exceptions:

- **Semester contribution:** every student pays an administration fee each semester, usually between about €100 and €400. It often includes a public transport ticket, which is good value.
- **Baden-Württemberg** (for example Stuttgart, Heidelberg, Freiburg, Karlsruhe) charges students from outside the EU **€1,500 per semester**.
- **Technical University of Munich (TUM)** charges tuition to non-EU students for most programs starting from 2024.
- Some **specialised Master's degrees**, especially "continuing education" or executive programs, charge fees.
- **Private universities** in Germany charge tuition, often €10,000 to €20,000 a year or more.

Always check the fees on the program's own page before you apply.

## The blocked account: the money you must show

To get a German student visa you need to prove you can pay your living costs. Most students do this with a **blocked account** (Sperrkonto): you deposit one year of living costs into a German account and can withdraw only a fixed amount each month after you arrive.

For 2026 the standard amount is **€11,904 for one year**, which is **€992 a month**. This figure has applied since late 2024 and is linked to the support rate for German students, so it can rise. Confirm the current figure with the German Embassy in Islamabad or the Consulate General in Karachi before you transfer money.

Other accepted proofs include a German scholarship or a formal declaration of commitment from someone living in Germany, but the blocked account is the most common route for Pakistani students.

!!! Good to know
The blocked account money is still yours. It pays your rent and food in your first year. It is not a fee.

## Living costs

The blocked account amount is roughly the minimum. Real monthly costs depend on the city:

- **Rent:** a room in a student residence or shared flat costs roughly €300 to €700, and more in Munich, Frankfurt or Hamburg. Student housing fills fast, so apply as soon as you are admitted.
- **Health insurance:** compulsory. Public student health insurance costs roughly €130 to €150 a month.
- **Food, phone and daily costs:** about €250 to €350 a month if you cook at home.

Smaller university cities such as Chemnitz, Magdeburg or Leipzig are noticeably cheaper than Munich or Frankfurt.

## Do you need German?

Not always. Germany has a large number of **English-taught Master's programs**, especially in engineering, computer science, data science, business and economics. You can search them on the official DAAD database at [daad.de](https://www.daad.de).

Most **Bachelor's programs** are taught in German, so they usually require German language skills, often at C1 level.

Even in an English program, learning German makes a big difference for part-time jobs, everyday life and finding a job after graduation. Starting German classes in Pakistan before you leave is one of the best investments you can make.

## Admission requirements in brief

- **Master's:** a relevant Bachelor's degree with good grades, usually a 16-year education, an IELTS or TOEFL score for English programs, a CV and a motivation letter. Some programs ask for the GRE or GMAT.
- **Bachelor's:** Pakistani FSc or A-levels alone are often not enough for direct entry. Many students first complete a foundation year (Studienkolleg) or one or two years of university study in Pakistan. Each university decides, so check the entry requirements carefully.
- Many universities receive applications through **uni-assist**, which checks your documents for a fee. Others take applications directly.

Deadlines for the Winter semester (starting in October) are often between March and July, and some programs close much earlier for non-EU applicants. Start a year in advance.

## The student visa from Pakistan

1. Get your admission letter.
2. Open and fund your blocked account, and get health insurance confirmation.
3. Book your national (D) visa appointment with the German mission in Pakistan. Waiting times can be long, so book as early as the rules allow.
4. Prepare attested degrees, your admission letter, proof of funds, language certificates, a CV and a motivation letter.

As with any visa, the decision belongs only to the embassy or consulate.

## Working during and after your studies

- International students can work **140 full days or 280 half days a year**, plus student assistant jobs at the university. This helps with living costs but should not be part of your visa funding plan.
- After graduation, you can stay for up to **18 months** to look for a job related to your degree. Graduates who find skilled work can move on to a work residence permit.

## Germany or the USA?

Germany is usually far cheaper, and the path to working there after your degree is clear. The USA offers more scholarship and assistantship money, more English-language life and very strong career networks in many fields.
